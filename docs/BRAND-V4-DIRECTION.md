# Racquet Habit v4 — Court Culture, Unbuttoned

Status: local design exploration only. Not approved for production or deployment.

## Thesis

Racquet Habit should feel like a designer tennis label made by people who
actually play: cultivated, kinetic, social, slightly strange, and visibly
human. The brand can keep the current site's excellent editorial typography
and direct commerce copy, but the identity and imagery need more pulse.

The central tension is:

- tournament composure versus post-match looseness;
- formal Roman typography versus handwritten interruption;
- premium styling versus surreal scale and physical comedy;
- precise court geometry versus ink, felt, chalk, and imperfect drawing.

The brand should never settle into a single retro era or imitate an established
tournament identity. It should use tennis as raw material rather than costume.

## Voice words

**Kinetic. Inked. Sun-struck.**

## What the moodboard is actually saying

### Keep

- decisive editorial typography;
- large negative space and strong cropping;
- direct, uncomplicated commerce;
- deep court green as a grounding color;
- dry copy rather than loud jokes.

### Introduce

- organic handwritten `Habit` lettering;
- a complete symbol with a memorable silhouette;
- exaggerated perspective and impossible tennis scale;
- fashion photography with playfulness and social energy;
- full-field blue, clay, orange, felt yellow, and green compositions;
- naïve racquet drawings and bold geometric court illustrations;
- intentional type/image collisions;
- one disruptive visual gesture per viewport.

### Retire

- the genteel all-serif wordmark;
- pale ivory as the default surface for the entire experience;
- polite, evenly paced editorial modules;
- photography that looks like generic luxury tennis stock;
- ornamental systems that need explanation before they feel ownable.

## Identity architecture

### Primary wordmark

Return to the stacked relationship:

```text
RACQUET
Habit
```

`RACQUET` remains controlled and elegant. `Habit` should look signed quickly
after a match: connected, confident, imperfect, and slightly oversized. The
handwriting should overlap, underline, or cut through the formal word rather
than sit politely underneath it.

### Symbol

The new logomark must work without letters and survive:

- 16 px favicon;
- 15 mm embroidery;
- one-color screen print;
- racquet dampener;
- cap patch;
- ball can and bottle cap.

The preferred conceptual territory is a repeated bounce or habit loop resolved
into one strong silhouette. It must not be a generic tennis ball, crossed
racquets, shield, wreath, or anonymous luxury monogram.

### RH monogram

Keep the founder-approved serif RH available as a formal secondary mark. It
should not carry the entire brand by itself.

## Color strategy

Use a full palette, but never every color at once.

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Club Green | `#0B3D2E` | identity anchor and commerce ink |
| True White | `#FFFFFF` | primary neutral and clean campaign field |
| Court Blue | `#2E7BEA` | digital court surface and editorial field |
| Deep Night | `#120A45` | graphic shadow and high-contrast alternate |
| Clay Orange | `#F04A1D` | energetic campaign field and artwork |
| Service Red | `#D63D1D` | accessible digital type, rules and controls |
| Felt Yellow | `#DFFF32` | bounce, action, and small conversion accent |
| Soft Sky | `#B9E8F5` | surreal photographic backdrop |
| Blush Court | `#F3A8B8` | rare collaboration or seasonal field |

Ordinary commerce uses Club Green, white, and one court color. Felt Yellow is
an event, not a site-wide highlighter.

## Typography

The user explicitly likes the current website typography and copy, so the live
type system remains structurally intact:

- Instrument Serif for editorial statements;
- Geist for interface and body copy;
- Roboto Condensed for genuine collection or production metadata.

The exception is the brand lettering: `Habit` returns to a custom handwritten
SVG rather than an italic serif. A licensed or bespoke final lettering pass is
still required before physical mass production.

For occasional campaign collisions, a heavy condensed grotesk may be used in
artwork—not as the commerce UI font.

## Signature gesture

The page is remembered by one elastic ball-flight trajectory that behaves like
handwriting: it can cross a photo, clip through a heading, become an underline,
or resolve into the symbol. It appears once per major page, not as wallpaper.

## Photography

- unusual lens or camera position;
- bodies in motion or caught between poses;
- full natural skin and fabric texture;
- direct flash, hard daylight, or clear sky color;
- confident styling without visible third-party logos;
- social casting and humor through composition;
- no cinematic teal grade, lonely luxury stare, or generic club portrait.

## Graphic art

Three compatible modes:

1. clean, saturated court geometry with one impossible scale shift;
2. naïve hand-drawn racquets, strings, notes, and score marks;
3. type/image collision using one oversized phrase or brush intervention.

They may coexist because the consistency comes from voice, palette, and tennis
materials—not from forcing every asset into one drawing style.

## Local website objective

The local redesign should prove:

- the restored handwritten wordmark;
- one selected full symbol/logomark;
- new hero and supporting campaign imagery;
- stronger color pacing;
- expressive art used on the homepage and selected merch previews;
- unchanged shopping clarity and accessible responsive behavior.

No production deployment or Fourthwall catalog mutation is authorized in this
phase.
