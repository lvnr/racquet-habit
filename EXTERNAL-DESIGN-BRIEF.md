# Racquet Habit — External Production Brief

The current identity and storefront establish the creative system: **Modern Ritual**. It is ceremonial, obsessive, precise, and deadpan. This brief identifies the specialist work that should be commissioned before the brand is treated as fully production-ready.

## 1. Brand mark and typography

Commission a senior identity designer or type designer to redraw the supplied RH Habit Loop and `RACQUET HABIT` wordmark as final optical masters.

Required deliverables:

- custom wordmark lettering, not an unchanged retail font
- refined Q tail and spacing across `RACQUET HABIT`
- small-size correction for 12–20 mm embroidery
- one-color, reversed, foil, deboss, woven, and stitch-tested variants
- master vector files in AI, SVG, EPS, and press-ready PDF
- spacing, minimum-size, and misuse rules
- verified thread counts and satin-stitch paths from an embroidery sample

The supplied SVG system is strong enough for launch prototypes and digital use. The last 10% must be judged on real thread, screen print, foil, and fabric—not only on a monitor.

## 2. Garment design and product development

Hire a technical apparel designer and a production partner for original cut-and-sew pieces. Avoid making the permanent line a sequence of generic blanks with graphics.

Required work:

- fit blocks for heavyweight tee, polo, crewneck, cap, and warm-up layer
- graded measurement specs and tech packs
- fabric weight, wash, shrinkage, colorfastness, and abrasion testing
- trim library: woven neck labels, care labels, zipper pulls, bartacks, rib, and thread
- Pantone TCX targets for Court Ink, Chalk, Oxblood, and Tennis Acid
- strike-offs, fit samples, size-set samples, and pre-production samples
- play-testing for movement, sweat, washing, and racquet-bag wear

Fourthwall products should be treated as a market-validation capsule. Keep successful silhouettes, then graduate hero products into controlled manufacturing.

## 3. Product and campaign photography

Replace the AI campaign image before a major paid launch with an original shoot. The generated image is an art-direction reference, not a substitute for owning authentic campaign material.

Commission:

- an art director or photographer experienced in fashion and sport
- one night-court campaign and one hard-daylight court setup
- on-body front, back, detail, and movement views for each garment
- consistent studio product views for every SKU and colorway
- macro photography of fabric, embroidery, seams, labels, and packaging
- a diverse casting approach that feels credible to real tennis players
- vertical, square, landscape, and transparent-background crops
- usage rights covering web, organic social, paid media, retail, press, and out-of-home

Visual treatment: warm highlights, deep green-black shadows, controlled grain, direct flash after sunset, and no generic country-club staging.

## 4. Packaging and physical identity

Hire a packaging designer with a structural supplier. The intended experience is a private-society record, not a standard printed mailer.

Required components:

- recycled mailer or box with verified manufacturing dielines
- string-lattice tissue or glassine
- tall oval hangtag and cotton cord specification
- numbered membership card system
- woven label and care-label artwork
- product-specific inserts for drinkware, coffee, and fragile goods
- foil/deboss/ink drawdowns using actual substrates
- CMYK, spot-color, foil, paper, board, and finish specifications
- transit, scuff, moisture, and drop tests

Use real muted-champagne foil or hardware for Warm Metal; do not imitate metal with flat yellow ink.

## 5. Drinkware, towels, and accessories

Source specialists rather than forcing every item through a print-on-demand catalog.

- Custom jacquard or dobby-woven towel with the lattice embedded in construction
- BPA-free reusable cup and insulated bottle with food-contact compliance documentation
- dishwasher, heat, coating, and scratch testing for drinkware
- custom racquet dampener, overgrip finishing tape, stencil card, and ball-can sleeve
- packaging engineered for dents, chips, leaking, and condensation
- physical samples approved under daylight and warm retail lighting

## 6. Coffee collaborations and seasonal drinks

Engage a licensed roaster/co-packer and food regulatory counsel before taking orders.

Required decisions and deliverables:

- roast profile, origin, minimum order, freshness window, and fulfillment responsibility
- sensory cupping and shelf-life testing
- lot code, roast/best-before date, net weight, origin, ingredient, allergen, and storage copy
- compliant labels for every market being shipped to
- food-contact packaging and valve specification
- nutrition and ingredient review for any prepared sports/seasonal drink
- cold-chain or local-pickup operating plan if the seasonal drink is sold prepared
- product-liability coverage and recall procedure

The limited roast can ship as a packaged product. A prepared seasonal drink should begin as a local collaboration or event item until operations and compliance are proven.

## 7. Legal, commerce, and accessibility review

Before meaningful sales volume:

- obtain trademark clearance and filing advice for the name, monogram, motto, and relevant product classes
- commission final terms, privacy, cookie, returns, shipping, duty, tax, and warranty policies
- confirm Armenian and international VAT/customs treatment with an accountant
- document Fourthwall as merchant/fulfillment provider wherever legally required
- perform an independent WCAG 2.2 AA audit, including keyboard and screen-reader testing
- conduct moderated checkout tests with users in Armenia, the US, and at least one EU market
- test refund, replacement, lost-parcel, and address-correction workflows end to end

## 8. Launch content and lifecycle

Produce a disciplined launch package rather than filling channels with templates:

- campaign film and short cutdowns
- product-detail copy grounded in actual material specifications
- launch editorial, manifesto, and founder story
- welcome, abandoned-bag, post-purchase, shipping, review, and replenishment email flows
- press kit with vector marks, founders’ note, photography, product facts, and credits
- analytics and consent implementation with a documented measurement plan

## Sign-off sequence

1. Trademark review and final identity drawing
2. Physical identity and embroidery/print tests
3. Fit, material, and product samples
4. Packaging prototypes and transit tests
5. Original photography and campaign production
6. Legal, tax, accessibility, and checkout review
7. Controlled launch, customer feedback, then production expansion

Do not approve a permanent production asset from a mockup alone. Every critical decision should be signed off from the actual substrate, garment, print, stitch, package, or product in hand.
