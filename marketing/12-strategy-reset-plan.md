# Performance-First Marketing Strategy

**Version 2 · July 28, 2026 · supersedes the earlier culture-first reset**

## The correction

Racquet Habit does not need to earn the right to sell later. It needs to sell now.

The social profiles still need enough substance to reassure a visitor that the brand is real, coherent, and worth buying from. That is a supporting job. The primary job is commercial:

> **Sell the life. Show the object. Ask for the order.**

The earlier reset over-weighted followability, real-world community, lunch, and “one more set.” Those can remain occasional details. They are not the center of the brand or a prerequisite for paid acquisition.

## The commercial idea

### Position

> **Racquet Habit makes court goods for a more beautiful sporting life: competitive, sun-struck, sensual, and slightly excessive.**

The product is a way into that life. We are not selling performance claims or inherited club status. We are selling taste, appetite, composure, and the pleasure of being dressed correctly for the day.

### The internal creative territory

**The private sporting life**

This is an internal direction, not a phrase that must appear in customer-facing copy. It means:

- beautiful access without fake aristocratic heritage;
- contemporary sport, not museum tennis;
- pleasure without forced comedy;
- attractive people who appear self-possessed rather than delighted to be in an ad;
- moments before, during, and after play;
- product desire with a clear path to purchase.

“Elite” should be expressed through taste, restraint, access, body language, location, casting, light, and edit rhythm. Do not write “elite,” “luxury,” “exclusive,” or “old money” on the creative. Those words weaken the effect.

### What the brand is not

- not a tennis-news or tennis-community publication;
- not a lunch club with merchandise;
- not a performancewear authority;
- not fake heritage or country-club cosplay;
- not generic influencer enthusiasm;
- not wall-to-wall jokes;
- not quiet luxury with the product hidden;
- not artificial scarcity, fake reviews, or invented customer experience.

## The buyer

The primary paid audience is a style-conscious racquet-life buyer, roughly 22–40, who treats sport as part of identity rather than an isolated workout.

They buy for four different reasons:

| Buying motive | What they want | What the creative must prove |
|---|---|---|
| Aspiration | access to the world and mood | the product belongs in a desirable life |
| Identification | a phrase or object that feels like them | the design is specific and legible |
| Styling | something wearable beyond the court | the garment works in a complete look |
| Gifting | an object that immediately reads as “them” | product, price, purpose, delivery, and trust are clear |

The buyer does not need to be an excellent player. They do need to understand the social and visual codes of racquet life.

## The persuasion sequence

Every conversion asset should do at least three of these four jobs:

1. **Desire:** make the viewer want the scene, person, or state of mind.
2. **Recognition:** make the product and design unmistakable.
3. **Proof:** show front, back, print, scale, styling, or use accurately.
4. **Action:** give the viewer a concrete reason and route to shop now.

Mood without product is a film-school exercise. Product without desire is a catalog listing. The winning creative combines both.

## Visual language

### Lane 1 — private sporting life

**Share of creative development: about 45%**

Professional fashion photography and film with a contemporary racquet setting:

- hard Mediterranean sun, warm late light, night-court floodlights, direct flash;
- clay, green hard court, hotel corridors, terraces, pools, locker rooms, parked cars, balconies, and quiet service spaces;
- close crops through strings, towels, glass, mirrors, fences, and doorways;
- low angles, long-lens observation, decisive negative space, and occasional wide-angle distortion;
- sweat, wet hair, flushed skin, tan lines, wrinkled garments, and surface texture;
- self-possessed expressions, private jokes, appetite, recovery, waiting, and departure;
- product visible early and often.

The hedonism comes from the ritual: cold condensation after heat, a post-match shower, bare feet on warm tile, night-court light, room service left open, fruit cut on a balcony, a towel still damp, a racquet in the back seat, a second outfit after play. Lunch can appear, but it is one ritual among many.

### Lane 2 — synthetic intimacy

**Share of creative development: about 30%**

Creator-camera language made with synthetic adult talent:

- mirror checks, handheld phone footage, bedroom or hotel-room styling, back-seat clips, bathroom light, court-bench changes, and walking shots;
- natural autofocus shifts, imperfect framing, plausible phone exposure, room tone, and small human hesitations;
- the product named or shown in the first two seconds;
- one clear styling, selection, or product-discovery premise;
- no generic “hey guys” performance and no over-enthusiastic review voice.

The craft should be convincing enough that the viewer is not distracted by AI artifacts. The provenance still follows the disclosure rules of the platform.

### Lane 3 — product command

**Share of creative development: about 25%**

Direct product and offer creative:

- authoritative front/back/detail carousels;
- clean product-on-model frames;
- one product per frame when recognition matters;
- concise price, collection, shipping, or offer information;
- simple “Shop now” language;
- catalog and dynamic product formats for retargeting.

This lane does not need to mimic an editorial. Its job is to close the information gap created by the more atmospheric work.

## Non-negotiable visual rules

1. Product truth comes only from each product’s `catalog/` directory.
2. Product-specific `editorial-court-archive/` images guide art direction, not product construction.
3. Identity assets come from `design/brand-fixed/identity/`; use `design/brand-fixed/assets/` selectively.
4. User-supplied professional references and `Downloads/cinematic-inspiration` inform light, composition, lens, color, gesture, and edit rhythm only.
5. Use modern graphite/composite racquets in all active play and court photography.
6. Wooden racquets may appear sparingly in a still life, stationery scene, or archive prop. Never put one in a player’s hand on court.
7. Preserve pores, fabric behavior, asymmetry, sweat, environmental wear, and plausible anatomy.
8. Do not add generated typography, logos, labels, embroidery, packaging, or product details to a final asset.
9. One strong status signal per scene is enough. Avoid a pile of champagne, convertibles, watches, and hotel keys.
10. No fake aged-film filter pasted over every image. Choose a photographic process per treatment.

## Synthetic talent policy

Anonymous AI fashion models are a core production tool, not a fallback.

Build a small house cast:

- two women and one man, all clearly adult;
- distinct faces, bodies, hair, and energy;
- fashion-editorial rather than stock-model attractiveness;
- repeatable reference sets for continuity;
- no biography, fake follower count, or claim that the person is a real customer.

Use them for:

- campaign editorials;
- styling and try-on sequences;
- product demonstrations;
- creator-style ads;
- fashion films;
- visual hooks that would be expensive to shoot conventionally.

Use Ani when her real role as cofounder matters and she approves the use. Hold Marc as a backup character; anonymous house talent is better for the first commercial batch.

### The disclosure line

We can make synthetic creative visually natural. We cannot build a strategy around deceiving the viewer about a customer experience.

Allowed:

- “Three ways I’d wear the Signed Rally tee.”
- “Court clothes for the part of the day that is not the match.”
- a mirror selection between two real products;
- a synthetic model wearing or handling a faithful product;
- a creator-style voice that describes what is visibly true.

Do not publish:

- “I bought this,” “mine just arrived,” or “I’ve worn this for weeks” when no such purchase or use happened;
- fake reviews, ratings, comments, delivery footage, or customer screenshots;
- claims about fit, hand-feel, durability, shipping speed, or packaging that have not been verified;
- an unboxing before a real Fourthwall sample establishes the actual experience.

TikTok requires an AIGC label or clear disclosure for significantly AI-generated ads. Meta is rolling AI information labels into ad transparency and says it is detecting third-party AI signals. The correct target is **indistinguishable in craft, transparent in provenance**.

## The content system

### 1. Campaign worlds

High-impact fashion stories that sell the collection through one controlled fantasy.

Examples:

- **Private Hours:** a late-afternoon court, hotel corridor, pool, and night arrival; the product follows the person through the day.
- **Heat Policy:** hard sun, court exertion, cold surfaces, condensation, towel, and recovery.
- **After the Match:** shower steam, wet hair, an oversized tee, balcony air, racquet in the corner.
- **Night Service:** floodlights, empty stands, a parked car, hotel lift, direct flash.
- **Room 6–0:** a room-key motif and a complete product look; no fake guest story.

Each campaign world produces a hero film, stills, direct-response cutdowns, product crops, and a landing-page visual.

### 2. Dressed to play

Product-led styling with synthetic talent:

- one tee, three complete looks;
- crop versus oversized;
- court-to-evening transition;
- choose the cap, tote, or case that completes the look;
- front/back turn;
- close-up print and silhouette;
- paired-product outfits.

This is consideration content with a direct shop action, not a fashion tutorial detached from the catalog.

### 3. Private rituals

Short scenes of appetite, preparation, recovery, and pleasure:

- cold drink after hot court;
- changing in the car or hotel room;
- fruit, ice, water, towel, shower, balcony, room key, late check-out;
- night-court arrival;
- tying shoes, removing jewelry, reapplying sunscreen, wetting hair;
- setting a racquet down and putting on the oversized tee.

The ritual must be anchored to a real product. Lunch and “one more set” can appear when they sharpen a particular idea; they no longer define the system.

### 4. Product proof

Commercial publishing that answers buying questions:

- product front/back/details;
- exact design close-up;
- verified sizes and variants;
- price and shipping information pulled live;
- how two products compare;
- what belongs in each capsule;
- giftable object edits;
- customer proof only when real orders and real customers exist.

## Platform roles

### Instagram

The profile is the brand’s visual storefront.

- Seed six strong posts before or alongside the first ad traffic, not a 12-post mosaic.
- Keep the grid rich in product, faces, and movement.
- Use Reels and carousels as sales formats, not only reach formats.
- Pin a brand-world hero, a shop-the-collection post, and a product-proof post.
- Use Stories for direct links, product choices, live price, shipping reminders, and retargetable engagement.

### Facebook

Facebook is paid distribution and trust infrastructure.

- Run the same Meta sales system across Facebook and Instagram placements.
- Keep the Page complete, current, and visibly shoppable.
- Post the strongest campaign films, offer units, and proof carousels.
- Do not spend time manufacturing a separate editorial voice at launch.

### TikTok

TikTok is the creator-style conversion laboratory.

- Publish natural synthetic creator-style posts and run paid variants quickly.
- Put the product or outcome in the opening frame.
- Build each video around a purchase-relevant premise: selection, styling, identity, proof, or availability.
- Use the commercial-content setting and AIGC disclosure where required.
- Do not imitate a real creator’s identity, voice, or testimonial.

### Pinterest

Pinterest is visual search and shopping capture.

- Publish catalog Pins for all 24 products.
- Build campaign, styling, and gift Pins around the six hero products.
- Use direct product destinations and current metadata.
- Add paid shopping only after the catalog, tag, and product URLs are verified.
- It is a conversion channel, not a resized Instagram archive.

## Paid acquisition from day one

### Prerequisites

Do not use a traffic or engagement campaign as a warm-up. Verify:

- Meta Pixel and Conversions API events;
- `ViewContent`, `AddToCart`, `InitiateCheckout`, and `Purchase`;
- correct value and currency;
- domain and checkout flow;
- product catalog and destination links;
- privacy/consent implementation;
- mobile product-page speed;
- shipping, returns, and support information;
- clean UTMs and analytics.

### Launch structure

At the current proposed budget, begin with one consolidated Meta Sales campaign:

- objective: Sales;
- conversion location: website;
- optimization event: Purchase when tracking is healthy;
- audience: broad US prospecting, with sensible exclusions for existing purchasers;
- placements: Advantage+ placements, with placement-specific 9:16, 4:5, and 1:1 assets;
- initial creative: eight materially different ads;
- budget: concentrate spend rather than fragmenting it across many small ad sets.

Do not create a separate retargeting campaign on day one if the audience is too small. Add catalog retargeting after traffic and product-view volume exist.

At the lower end of the monthly budget, keep acquisition concentrated on Meta. At the upper end, use TikTok and Pinterest as bounded creative tests after their pixels/tags and catalogs are ready.

| A1 | Private Hours hero | 15s 9:16 film | Signed Rally oversized tee | buy the uniform for the life |
| A2 | Heat Policy | 4:5 still / 8s cut | Love Cherries | graphic desire and summer styling |

Each concept needs at least two hooks, but do not mistake different first lines for different concepts.

### Offer

Do not default to a permanent discount. First test the product and the world at full price.

If conversion needs help, choose one margin-backed device:

- a real launch code with a fixed end date;
- a verified shipping offer;
- a product bundle;
- a gift-with-purchase that actually exists;
- an honest drop deadline.

Never imply low inventory or an ending collection when print-on-demand availability does not support the claim.

## Creative testing

Test arguments, not decoration.

The first variables are:

1. campaign-world desire versus creator intimacy versus product proof;
2. product family;
3. identity hook versus styling hook versus object/gift hook;
4. direct product page versus curated collection destination;
5. full-price message versus one verified offer.

Use spend relative to the allowable acquisition cost to make decisions. Do not kill an ad because its like count is low or scale one because it looks good in the feed.

Primary measures:

- purchases and contribution margin;
- customer acquisition cost;
- conversion value and MER/ROAS;
- landing-page conversion rate;
- average order value.

Diagnostic measures:

- outbound click-through rate;
- landing-page views per click;
- add-to-cart and checkout rate;
- first-two-second and six-second video hold;
- product-page exits;
- creative-level frequency and fatigue.

### Stage 1 — still treatments

Generate and approve 12 masters before expensive motion:

1. Private Hours hero, Signed Rally oversized tee
2. Private Hours close product frame
3. Heat Policy hero, Love Cherries
4. Heat Policy graphic/detail frame
5. After the Match hero, Emotional Support Racquet tee
6. After the Match hotel/balcony frame
7. Out of Office Court Cap identity portrait
8. Something Cold tote ritual frame
9. Serve Chilled product ritual frame
10. synthetic mirror-styling keyframe
11. synthetic creator close-detail keyframe
12. six-product Start Here commerce layout

For each master:

- one written treatment;
- one coherent reference board;
- relevant catalog inputs;
- relevant Court Archive style inputs;
- 4:5 composition;
- 9:16-safe alternate or extension;
- product-fidelity and anatomy review;
- approval before animation.

#### Approved reference-first native workflow

For professional campaign stills, the current first-choice development method is:

1. select one photographic reference that already solves the intended composition, casting, light, color, gesture, and mood;
2. supply the relevant current `catalog/` front and/or back image separately as product truth;
3. generate a new image directly with native image generation, explicitly labeling the catalog image as product truth and the photographic image as art direction only;
4. keep the prompt short and describe only the deliberate differences from the reference;
5. inspect the result at full resolution for product artwork, anatomy, racket type, generated microtexture, and facial plausibility;
6. allow one targeted retry only when the defect is specific and correctable.

Pinterest campaign photography and the approved professional-reference archive are the preferred discovery sources. Use one close reference per master rather than averaging several unrelated mood images. Do not copy the reference’s person, brand, garment, logo, or text.

The Recraft-plate → full-frame native product-edit pipeline is not approved for this library. In testing, the edit repainted the complete photograph and weakened Recraft’s human and surface realism. Recraft may still be used for unbranded ideation or when product artwork can be applied without generatively redrawing the person.

### Stage 2 — motion after still approval

Animate or produce:

1. Private Hours 15s hero
2. Private Hours 6s product cut
3. Heat Policy 8s film
4. After the Match 10s film
5. three-ways creator video
6. mirror-selection creator video
7. six-product catalog cut
8. cap/tote/tumbler object cut

Use Higgsfield for reference-led motion and product/character workflows. Use native image generation for economical still development where it can preserve the required product truth. Model choice happens treatment by treatment; do not force every asset through one model.

### Stage 3 — deterministic finishing

- real logo and typography;
- color and grain matching;
- price and offer pulled live;
- captions and subtitles;
- platform safe zones;
- disclosure controls;
- product URL and UTM;
- export and contact-sheet QA.

## Ten-day launch sequence

### Days 0–2

- verify tracking, catalog, product pages, shipping, returns, and live prices;
- approve the six hero products;
- approve the first 12 still treatments;
- publish the first three profile posts.

### Day 3

- publish the remaining three seed posts;
- launch the consolidated Meta Sales campaign with the eight-ad matrix;
- begin clean creative-level reporting.

### Days 4–6

- inspect funnel behavior, not vanity engagement;
- fix broken destinations, weak product pages, or tracking immediately;
- publish one product-proof post and one creator-style post;
- prepare motion only from approved stills.

### Day 7

- compare product, argument, and format performance;
- pause only when spend and funnel evidence justify it;
- produce new versions of the winning argument;
- add catalog retargeting only if audience volume supports it.

### Days 8–10

- launch the first TikTok test if the pixel, disclosures, and creative are ready;
- publish Pinterest product and styling Pins;
- expand the winning hero product into additional hooks and landing-page treatments.

## Approval gates

1. **Commercial strategy:** position, buyer, hero products, and opening eight-ad matrix.
2. **Visual direction:** one reference board for each of the three visual lanes.
3. **Stills:** approve product fidelity, casting, mood, and composition.
4. **Motion:** approve beat sheet, duration, sound, and sales message.
5. **Offer and landing page:** verify every claim, destination, price, and shipping statement.
6. **Launch:** publish profile seed and start the Sales campaign without an engagement warm-up.
7. **Scale:** make more of the winning argument, not merely more content.

- [Meta creative diversification](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/ad-creative): recommends varied formats, placements, and creative concepts.
- [Meta Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads): recommends vertical 9:16 video with audio and key messages inside safe zones.
- [Meta’s June 2026 AI-ad transparency update](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/): expands AI information to ads shaped by third-party tools.
- [TikTok misleading-content and AIGC policy](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-misleading-and-false-content?lang=en): permits significant AI generation when labeled and rejects undisclosed AIGC.
- [TikTok ad disclaimers](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager): identifies AI-generated, synthetic, or manipulated media as requiring a disclaimer.
- [Pinterest Path to Performance](https://business.pinterest.com/pdf/pinterest-presents/path-to-performance/): current catalog and performance-creative guidance.

## Decision

Approve this direction before generating the next library. Once approved, the immediate creative deliverable is the three-lane visual-direction packet and the 12 still treatments. Motion follows only after the stills pass product-fidelity and taste review.
